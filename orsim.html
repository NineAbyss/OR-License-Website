ORSIM IN PRACTICE

From a brief to a better-informed plan.

Follow an agent as it asks, models, codes, and checks a small production schedule. ORSim supplies the missing business facts along the way.

AUTHORED DEMO
The starting brief

Three batches. One morning shift.

“Prepare a timetable for today’s food-production batches using our mixer and packer. Clarify the operating requirements before delivering the plan.”

A fictional, simplified scenarioInspired by Case 02: short-horizon food production.

All dialogue, numbers, and DEMO IDs below are invented for explanation. They are not the benchmark’s exact rules, a recorded trajectory, or measured results.

01

Elicitation · Turn 1 of 7

Start with the business goal

AAgent

Which batches must we produce, when are they due, and what matters most?

ORORSim

Produce three indivisible 100 kg batches: A1 and A2 of product A, and B1 of product B. A1 and A2 must finish packing by 10:00; B1 must finish by 11:00. All three are mandatory. Among timetables that meet those requirements, the business prefers the earliest completion of the last batch.

The agent learns what the business needs. Choosing a mathematical objective is still the agent’s job.

Elicitation · Turn 2 of 7

Establish the production process

AAgent

What equipment does each batch use, how long does each operation take, and when is the equipment available?

ORORSim

There is one mixer and one packer, both available throughout 08:00–12:00. Each batch requires 30 minutes of mixing followed by 30 minutes of packing. Each machine handles only one operation at a time; an operation cannot be interrupted. There is no initial setup or transport time.

A business description becomes concrete enough for the agent to start modeling resources and time.

03

Modeling · Turn 3 of 7

Correct a convenient assumption

AAgent

So I can run product B immediately after product A on the mixer, just as I can run A2 immediately after A1?

ORORSim

No. Switching products in either direction requires 15 minutes of cleaning on the mixer. Cleaning occupies the mixer and must finish before the next product starts mixing. There is no extra turnaround between batches of the same product, and the packer needs no product-change cleaning.

A leading question does not turn an incorrect assumption into a client fact.

05

Implementation · Turn 5 of 7

Resolve a timing detail while coding

AAgent

How long may a mixed batch wait for packing? Can a new operation start at the exact minute the previous one ends?

ORORSim

Packing must start between 0 and 30 minutes after mixing finishes, including both endpoints. A machine is free at an operation’s end time, so another operation may start at that same time. The same endpoint rule applies to mixer cleaning.

Clarification continues during implementation; it is not restricted to the initial interview.

06

Checking · Turn 6 of 7

Revisit a rule during a self-check

AAgent

I’m checking my draft: A2 mixes 08:30–09:00, B1 mixes 09:00–09:30, and I placed cleaning on the packer at 09:30–09:45. Does the cleaning belong on the packer?

ORORSim

No. Product-change cleaning occupies the mixer, not the packer. The 15-minute cleaning must be completed before mixing starts on the next product. The packer needs no product-change cleaning.

The agent’s own check prompts the question. It must detect the conflict and revise its timetable itself.
